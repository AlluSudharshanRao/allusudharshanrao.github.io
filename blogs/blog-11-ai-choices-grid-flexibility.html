AI model choices and grid flexibility

Part 11 · Model and deployment choices shift the feasible power range—and therefore how much grid coordination is even possible [1, §5].

Same 3 MW cap, different flexibility

OpenG2G scales replica counts so peak power hits ~3 MW at the largest latency-safe batch. Then it varies only the AI configuration. Any change in feasible range comes from the stack, not from “buying more capacity.”

Model size and architecture

On B200 GPUs, five models ordered by feasible range (widest first) tell a clear story [1, Fig. 5]:

  • Qwen3 8B — widest span; OFO drives violation below 1 pu·s.
  • Qwen3 32B — latency target caps max batch at 128 vs 256 on 8B, lowering the ceiling.
  • Qwen3 30B A3B (MoE) — only ~3B active params but routing keeps GPUs busier at small batch, raising the power floor.
  • GPT-OSS 120B — large dense stack, narrower span.
  • Qwen3 235B A22B — even min batch draws high power; throughput collapses vs smaller models.

Precision: BF16 vs FP8

For Qwen3 235B on 8× H100, decode at small batch is memory-bound—tensor cores under-used, power sits low. FP8 halves weight bytes, raises arithmetic intensity, and lifts the power floor. BF16 sees ~100× violation drop under OFO vs ~2× for FP8 because BF16 had more headroom to shed load [1, Fig. 6]. Faster inference is not always more grid-friendly.

Parallelism

Expert parallelism changes feasible batch range. Doubling parallelism on GPT-OSS 120B widens power span noticeably; the same change on Qwen3 235B barely moves it—architecture matters as much as GPU count [1, Fig. 7].

Design takeaways

  1. Co-design serving and interconnection. Pick model, precision, and parallelism knowing they set the MW span you can offer the utility.
  2. Simulate before you promise. OpenG2G lets you replay measured inference against IEEE feeders and compare droop, OFO, and PPO on identical scenarios [1].
  3. Keep measuring. Parts 1–7 emphasized traces; OpenG2G grounds coordination in the same ML.ENERGY style data.

Open source: github.com/gpu2grid/openg2g.

Series close. Parts 1–7 explained what AI loads do to power. Parts 8–11 explain how measured inference and grid simulators can close the loop when operators need flexibility without shutting off chatbots.
